# 12 — Serial quantized inference, bulk embeddings, large-scale linear algebra

**Status: testnet prototype, not audited.** This document describes what was built and **measured** for the three targeted workloads (inference of quantized models, bulk embedding computation, large-scale linear algebra), with their limits. The labels `[PROVEN]` (tested and replayed by a second implementation), `[MEASURED]` (reproducible figure on the development VPS: NVIDIA A10 GPU, 30 cores), `[HYPOTHESIS]`, `[RESEARCH]` are those of the rest of the file. Everything is reproducible with the cited scripts.

## 1. What was added

| component | where | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| **model registry** (per worker) | `net.rs`: `PutModel`, `HasModel`, `PutProgRef` | a model's weights are deposited **once** at the worker, then found by their Merkle root (the one already committed on-chain in `w_roots`); a job now only sends its input. Disk persistence, eviction of the sets for which no job is alive any more. |
| **serial inference / embeddings** | `vearl-cli infer` | one job per batch of samples of the model; up to N jobs in flight; each job verified operation by operation (Freivalds + recomputation) before payment; int8 output `[samples, dimensions]`. |

| **Merkle roots on the GPU** | `gpu::merkle_root_i32`, `work::c_root` | same tree as `crypto.rs` (leaf = blake3 `derive_key`, one block), root only; equality test with the CPU up to 2²⁰ leaves, including sizes that are not powers of two. |

| **`Bias` and `Lut` operations** + **PyTorch import** | `prog.rs` (`Op::Bias`, `Op::Lut`, `ProgFraud::Bias`, `ProgFraud::Lut`), `docs/07` §12, `tools/quantize.py` (`torch`, `selftest-torch`) | a bias vector and a 128-entry activation table (GELU, SiLU, tanh, sigmoid) are rows of a weight block; a PyTorch MLP with **biases, BatchNorm1d (folded) and an activation of one's choice** is imported from a `state_dict` (never a pickled model) and verified. Fifth and sixth fraud-proof types, replayed by a second implementation (§4). |

## 2. Bulk text embeddings `[MEASURED]`

**What this is, plainly.** It is **not** MiniLM that runs on the chain. The network runs a much smaller *student* (a bag of 1,024 tf-idf-weighted words → MLP 2,048 → 384 dimensions, no biases, ReLU), trained by distillation (cosine loss, 120,000 AG News texts) to imitate MiniLM's embeddings, then int8-quantized. It was chosen because it is expressed **exactly** with the protocol's verifiable operations. A real transformer (multi-head attention, GELU, affine LayerNorm, biases, 128-token sequences) would need operations and a quantization (per channel / per group) that were not there at that stage: `[RESEARCH]` (done afterwards: §4 ter to §4 octies).

**Quality** (7,168 AG News test texts, 4 categories; "nearest neighbour of the same category" = for each text, is its nearest neighbour by cosine of the same category, with no training label whatsoever):

| representation | nearest-neighbour accuracy |
|---|---|
| MiniLM (the teacher, **not** run by the chain) | 85.73 % |
| float student | 82.25 % |
| **int8 student, chain output (verified)** | **82.16 %** |
| raw TF-IDF on the same 1,024 words (reference with no learning) | 73.98 % |

The mean student/teacher cosine is only 0.67: the student does not reproduce MiniLM, it captures the bulk of the thematic structure. The int8 quantization costs 0.1 points (int8/float cosine: 0.989).

**Exactness.** The chain output (7,168 vectors of 384 dimensions, run by a real GPU worker) is **byte-for-byte identical** to that of the independent Python reference engine `[PROVEN]`.

**Throughput** (one A10 worker, jobs of 1,024 texts, 5 operations including 4 GEMMs, 20,480 texts = 20 jobs):

| setting | time | throughput |
|---|---|---|
| almost instantaneous blocks (`DIVISOR=64`), 4 jobs in flight | 34.0 s | **597 embeddings/s** |
| blocks of about 30 s (`DIVISOR=2·10⁹`), 16 jobs in flight | 171 s | **119 embeddings/s** |
| same, 32 jobs in flight, worker mempool at 64 transactions per sender | 141.6 s | **145 embeddings/s** |
| same but mempool at 16 transactions per sender (old value) | 377 s | 54 embeddings/s |

With realistic blocks, **it is the latency of the chain (claim, publication, acceptance: a few blocks), not the computation, that bounds the throughput**; it rises with the number of jobs in flight (limited by the worker's store: 32 live sets) **and** by the mempool policy: a worker publishes 6 transactions per 5-operation job (one claim, five `PostOp`s), and the limit of 16 pending transactions per sender slowed everything down (54/s); it was raised to 64 (a node policy, not consensus). The worker now publishes all the operations of a job in **a single transaction** (`PostBatch`, `docs/07` §12.5: 6 transactions per job reduced to 2), and the bound of a program rises from 64 to 240 operations and from 32 to 64 weight blocks; with 30 s blocks, 126 embeddings/s measured with this change (within the 119-145 variance: the number of blocks dominates). Since block times are random (exponential law of mean 30 s), measurements with realistic blocks vary from one run to another (119 and 145/s here). The weights (4 blocks, 4 MiB) are sent only once for the 20 jobs. Economic security: escrow at the floor (`MIN_ESCROW`, 1 VRL per GEMM) = 4 VRL per batch of 1,024 texts, i.e. ≈ 3,900 VRL per million embeddings **at the testnet floor price, which means nothing outside the testnet**.

**Student variants tried, with no gain**: with biases (`Bias`) and GELU / tanh activation (`Lut`), the nearest neighbour falls to 80.4-80.5 % (int8), against 82.2 % for the ReLU model without biases; ReLU with biases: 82.0 %. Replacing 384 of the 1,024 words with the most frequent bigrams (`--bigrams 384`) also **lowers** the nearest neighbour (81.0 % int8): the loss of the removed words exceeds the gain of context. The bottleneck is the number of input features, not the shape of the MLP: a vocabulary of 4,096 words (float, no quantization) raises the nearest neighbour from 82.3 % to 85.5 % (student/teacher cosine 0.67 → 0.78). Hence the **wide input** below.

**Wide input (more than `n` features)** `[PROVEN]` `[MEASURED]`: new operation `Map Shift{dr, dc}` (`out[i][j] = in[i+dr][j+dc]`, `docs/07` §12.2). The `nin` feature blocks of a batch are stored side by side in the columns of `T_0` (block `b`: columns `[b·B, (b+1)·B)`, `B = n / nin` samples per job); `Shift` extracts each block, one `Gemm` per block, `Add`. `quantize_mlp` does it by itself as soon as the input exceeds `n`. **4,096-word** model (`train_embed.py --vocab 4096`, `models/embed-agnews-v4096.json`): 20 operations including 10 GEMMs, 256 texts per job. On 7,424 test texts: nearest neighbour **84.86 % int8** (85.29 % float, MiniLM 86.03 %, 4,096-word TF-IDF 81.0 %), int8/float cosine 0.955 (the sum of 4 requantized partial products costs precision), chain output **byte-for-byte identical** to the Python engine; similarity search verified 84.3 %. Throughput on the GPU: **101 embeddings/s** (almost instantaneous blocks, 37 jobs; client-side verification, 1.9 s per job, dominates), against 597/s for the 1,024-word model: **2.6 quality points for ~6× less throughput**, to be chosen according to the use. On the 8 hand-written sentences the 4,096-word model finds a neighbour of the same theme for 6 out of 8 (against 8 out of 8 for the 1,024-word model): the sample is too small to conclude.

**Embedding client** (`tools/embed_client.py`, `EmbedClient.embed(texts)`): tokenizes and weights on the client side (`embed_text.py`), forms batches of 1,024 texts, calls `vearl-cli infer`, returns `(N, 384)` verified int8. On 8 **hand-written** sentences, outside the training set (world, sport, economy, technology), the nearest neighbour of each sentence has the same theme for **8 out of 8** (`python3 tools/embed_client_demo.py MODEL.json`, 4 s end to end).

**Similarity search at scale** (linear algebra, §3): `S = Q·Dᵀ` with 1,024 queries × 7,168 documents (embeddings normalized then quantized in [−64, 64]), computed by **one tiled job** (7 products), verified, paid; identical to numpy `[PROVEN]`; the nearest document is of the same category for 81.64 % of the queries (against 82.16 % with the exact embeddings: negligible similarity quantization loss). Reproduce: `python3 tools/train_embed.py /tmp/embed-agnews.json` (downloads MiniLM and AG News, ~10 min on 30 CPU cores), then `python3 tools/embed_demo.py /tmp/embed-agnews.json 20`.

## 3. Large-scale linear algebra `[MEASURED]`

Product `C = A·B` of 8-bit integers, on an `n = 1024` chain with a GPU worker, **end to end** (deposit, submission, computation, publication of the roots, download and verification, payment); the result is compared with an exact reference product (torch float64): **bit-for-bit identical** in every case.

| size | blocks | operations | deposit | worker computation (including chain latency) | client verification | total | downloaded |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2048³ | 8 | 17 GOP | 0.3 s | 3.8 s | 0.3 s | 5.1 s | 32 MiB |
| 4096³ | 64 | 137 GOP | 0.7 s | 3.8 s | 0.8 s → **0.4 s** | 5.8 s → **5.5 s** | 64 MiB |
| **8192³** | 512 | **1,100 GOP** | 2.8 s | 6.2 s → 5.9 s | 2.7 s → **1.6 s** | 12.7 s → **11.3 s** | 256 MiB |

(Arrows: before → after the v2 row-wise commitment, `docs/07` §3. The "cheating worker" path is unchanged: wrong product located, `Product` proof accepted, job `Slashed`, `CHEAT="--cheat" python3 tools/la_demo.py 2 2 2`.)

On the worker side, for 8192³: 512 exact GEMMs in **2.0 s** (≈ 550 GOP/s with the PCIe transfers) and 576 Merkle roots in **0.15 s on CPU** with the row-wise commitment (1.7 s with the old per-entry commitment, even computed on the GPU; 4.6 s for 80 roots on CPU previously: the roots were the bottleneck). The per-entry GPU roots kernel is removed.

**Two measured design corrections.** (1) The Merkle roots computed on the CPU dominated (4.6 s for 80 roots of 10⁶ leaves): they move to the GPU (0.24 s). (2) The first version of the client downloaded each of the `I·J·K` products (2 GiB at 8192³) and computed their root: 46 s. The **fast path** only downloads the `I·J` sums `C_ij` (256 MiB) and verifies them with a Freivalds test on the **sum** of the `K` block products (`Σ_k A_ik·(B_kj·x) = C_ij·x`, 24 rounds, error ≤ 2⁻²⁴ per group): 2.7 s. The detailed path (download of the `K` products, localisation of the wrong product or sum, fraud proof) is only taken on failure; **tested with a real cheating worker**: wrong product → `Product` proof accepted, wrong sum → `Sum` proof accepted, job `Slashed` in both cases (`CHEAT="--cheat" python3 tools/la_demo.py 2 2 2`).

**Linear-algebra client** (`tools/la_client.py`, `LA.matmul(A, B)` / `gram(X)` / `similarity(Q, D)`): pads with zeros up to a multiple of 1,024, splits into jobs of at most 8 × 8 × 8 blocks, sums the jobs over K (exactly) and trims the result: matrices of any shape. **PCA of 20,480 embeddings** (`tools/pca_demo.py`): the 384 × 384 Gram matrix (K = 20,480 rows = 3 jobs of 8 + 8 + 4 blocks, each verified) is **identical to numpy**; explained variance 20 % (2 components), 42 % (8), 72.7 % (32), 87 % (64); the projection onto k components is also a verified job (V quantized in int8): nearest neighbour of the same category **79.5 % in 8 dimensions, 82.2 % in 32, 82.3 % in 64**, against 82.2 % with the original 384 dimensions.

**Honest limits (to be read before seeing an advantage in this):**
- Client and worker are here **on the same machine**: the local reference product (30 cores, float64) takes 1.1 s for 8192³, less than going through the chain (12.7 s). The value of verified outsourcing appears when the client **does not have** this power; a 4-core client takes of the order of 20-30 s for 1,100 GOP locally and cannot do it at all on a phone. Not measured here. `[HYPOTHESIS]`
- The cost of a verification is dominated by the **transfer** (4 MiB of sum per output block) and the Merkle roots (2 · 10⁶ hashes per output block on CPU), not by Freivalds.
- The minimum latency of a job is a few **blocks** (submission, claim, publication): with 30 s blocks, ~2-5 minutes whatever the size (measured: 300 s for a 7-product job with `DIVISOR=2·10⁹`); the block time is now 10 s, so about 7 blocks, i.e. ~70 s (measured on 2026-10-17: 225 s with 29-s blocks and 37 s with 5.5-s blocks for the same 39-operation job). Throughput is gained by launching many jobs in parallel, not by speeding up a job.
- 8-bit integers (|x| ≤ 64) are the domain: a floating-point computation must be quantized by the client (accuracy loss at their expense). There is **no** linear solver, decomposition or FFT: only products (and, by composing jobs, chains of products, Gram matrices, similarities). `[TO DO]`
- **Availability**: tiled jobs now have, like tensor programs, `Fetch` / `Serve` / `SlashUnserved` (§4 bis); `vearl-cli tiled-run` triggers the scenario by itself if the worker does not provide the sums.
- Security: `MAX_BLOCKS = 8` (8192³ at most); the worker keeps `I·K·J` products of 4 MiB in memory (2 GiB at 8192³).

## 4. Inference of quantized models: where we stand

`[PROVEN]` and `[MEASURED]` (see also `docs/11`): MLP 784→512→256→10 (98.12 % on 10,000 MNIST images in int8, against 98.20 % in float; 98.24 % measured on the real n = 1024 chain for the first 1,024), MLP 784→4096→4096→10 (layers split into blocks: 39 operations, 97.85 % on 1,024 images), CNN with residual blocks, transformer encoder block (14 operations: integer softmax and LayerNorm). This document adds: the model registry (weights are no longer sent again with each job), serial execution (`infer`), exact GEMM and Merkle roots on the GPU (MLP 784→4096→4096→10, 24 GEMMs: **11.3 s → 5.5 s** from submission to `Complete`, client verification 4.6 s → 3.7 s; MLP 784→512→256→10: 3.5 s and 1.9 s) and the embedding model.

**Biases and PyTorch import** `[PROVEN]` `[MEASURED]`: the new operation `Bias{x, w, row, relu}` adds a bias vector (a row of a weight block; a single block carries all of a network's biases): an MLP learned with biases is expressed `Gemm{relu: false}` then `Bias{relu}`. The quantizer rounds each bias to the scale of its layer's activations (`round(b / (sw · sx · 2^shift))`, saturated at ±64 and counted in the report) and calibrates the shift on `|z| + |b|`. On the digits (sklearn), MLP 64→48→10 learned with biases: **98.4 % float, 98.0 % int8**; PyTorch MLP with **biases and folded `BatchNorm1d`** (float gap after folding 3·10⁻⁶): **98.6 % float, 98.6 % int8** (`python3 tools/quantize.py selftest-torch`, shipped model `models/digits-mlp-bias.json`, test `shipped_digits_mlp_with_biases_and_folded_batchnorm_classifies_real_images`). Conformance: the independent Python operation engine is compared tensor by tensor with the Rust one on 3,000 random programs including ~2,000 `Bias` operations (0 divergence); the `Bias` fraud proof is accepted/refused identically by both validators (program differential fuzz: 6,000 decisions, 0 divergence; a bias MLP **defined, computed and proven wrong by the Python code** is accepted by the Rust code).

**Arbitrary activations (`Lut`)** `[PROVEN]` `[MEASURED]`: the table `table[k] = round(f((k − 64)·sa) / sy)` (`sa` = scale of the pre-activation, `sy = max|f| / 64` = output scale) is built by the quantizer. Same PyTorch MLPs (biases + BatchNorm), 98.4 % float: **GELU 98.4 % int8**, **tanh 99.0 % float / 99.2 % int8**, ReLU 98.6 % / 98.6 % (`python3 tools/quantize.py selftest-torch`, shipped model `models/digits-mlp-gelu.json`).

**Convolutional networks** `[PROVEN]` `[MEASURED]`: new operation `Pool Max` (maximum over `k×k` windows, compared with the independent Python engine on 3,000 random programs, 0 divergence; fraud proof = that of the element-wise operations) and `tools/quantize_cnn.py`: a PyTorch CNN ("same" convolutions of stride 1, folded `BatchNorm2d`, biases, ReLU/GELU/tanh, max or average pooling, flattening, dense layers) is imported from a `state_dict` with an architecture description (`--arch "conv,bn,relu,maxpool2,conv,relu,maxpool2,flatten,linear"`). A job processes `n // (h·w)` images (28×28 on `n = 1024`: only one; 8×8 on `n = 128`: two). Measured on the 8×8 digits: CNN with 2 convolutions, BatchNorm and 2 max-poolings, **98.8 % float, 98.8 % int8**; on a real `n = 128` chain, 128 images in 64 verified jobs: 98.4 % (`python3 tools/cnn_demo.py`, shipped model `models/digits-cnn-maxpool.json`).

**§4 bis — data availability of a program** `[PROVEN]` `[MEASURED]`: the hole was real: a worker that publishes its roots but withholds the tensors is **paid when the window expires** (the client can verify nothing). Three transactions fill the hole (specified in `docs/07` §12.5, same rules as simple-product jobs): `Fetch` (the client demands a column of a published tensor, fee set aside), `Serve` (the worker gives it with its Merkle path, the window is extended), `SlashUnserved` (with no answer after `serve_secs`, anyone sanctions: half of the bond to the reporter, half burned, escrow returned). `Settle` is blocked while a request is pending. Measured on real nodes: a test worker that withholds its results (`--no-serve`) is **sanctioned** (`python3 tools/da_demo.py`: `Fetch`, wait, `SlashUnserved`, job `Slashed`), and an honest worker serves the requested column by itself (test `program_data_request_is_served_on_chain_by_an_honest_worker`). `vearl-cli infer` and `vearl-cli tiled-run` trigger this scenario by themselves when a worker does not provide the data (`da_demo.py` shows it for both, jobs `Slashed`); tiled jobs request a product or sum entry (`docs/07` §13.3), test `tiled_data_requests_are_served_on_chain_by_an_honest_worker`. **Limit**: the guarantee is per column: a worker that withholds everything is punished from the first unserved request; one that serves the columns on-chain cannot deprive the client of what it asked for, but a client that wants a whole tensor through this route would pay `n` requests (it is the ultimate recourse, not the normal path).

**General convolutions** `[PROVEN]` `[MEASURED]`: new operation `Map Conv` (any stride and padding: `docs/07` §12.2), compared with the Python engine on ~4,000 random programs and with a naive convolution (test), element-wise fraud proof; `quantize_cnn.py` uses it for `conv/s2/p0`. Measured: CNN with a convolution without padding then of stride 2 with BatchNorm, on `n = 256` (4 images per job): **99.0 % float, 99.0 % int8** (`models/digits-cnn-stride.json`).

**ONNX import** `[MEASURED]`: `tools/import_onnx.py` reads a **sequential** graph (Conv with symmetric stride and padding, folded BatchNormalization, Relu/Gelu/Tanh/Sigmoid, MaxPool/AveragePool with window = stride, Flatten, Gemm or MatMul + Add) and converts it with the same quantizers. Any other operator (branches, residuals, attention, LayerNormalization…) is **refused, saying so**, never silently approximated. Measured on PyTorch models exported to ONNX: CNN (BatchNorm, GELU, max-pooling, stride-2 convolution) **98.8 % float / 99.0 % int8**; MLP with biases and tanh **97.8 % / 97.8 %**; the import is exact in float (gap ≤ 5·10⁻⁶) (`python3 tools/import_onnx.py selftest`).

**What is not done** `[TO DO]` / `[RESEARCH]`: ONNX import with branches (the quantizer with scale groups exists, §4 quater, but the ONNX import does not yet build its graphs), per-channel or per-group quantization (needed for large language models: LLM outliers fall outside the per-tensor int8 scale), attention mask (several sequences per job; multi-head attention has existed since §4 quinquies), tensors larger than `n × n` as the input of a GEMM. A 7-billion-parameter LLM does not fit in the current bounds (64 operations, 32 weight blocks of 1 MiB at the time of writing) and the protocol requires the verifier to know the weights (Freivalds test): an **on-chain** model registry and paid third-party auditors would be needed. The current registry is **per worker** (off-chain), not a consensus object.

## 4 ter. A small TRANSFORMER, trained, run and verified `[MEASURED]`

Until then "attention" only existed in a toy program with random weights. It now runs with **real learned weights**: `tools/train_tiny_transformer.py` trains, by distillation from MiniLM (in one minute of CPU), a tiny transformer — 1 layer, 1 head, `d = 64`, FFN 128, 32 tokens, vocabulary of 8,000 words, output of 128 dimensions — whose **topology is exactly the one the chain can run** (`tools/quantize_transformer.py`): input projection, LayerNorm, Q/K/V, `KᵀQ` scores (`GemmAT`), integer softmax, `V·A` (`GemmAT`), output projection, residual (`Add`), LayerNorm, ReLU FFN, residual, LayerNorm, sum of the tokens, projection. **20 operations including 11 GEMMs, 9 weight blocks, chain `n = 128`** (testnet parameters).

- **Quantization.** The scales are not guessed: they are **computed** from the real distributions (99.9th percentile) under the constraints of the integer format: the two branches of a residual have the same scale (the scale of the output projection's weights is adjusted so that the output falls exactly on the residual stream's scale), and the product of the scales of K and Q is adjusted so that the integer scores are expressed in notches of the integer softmax (`exp2(−d/8)`: one notch = ln 2 / 8 of logit). No floating point in the program.
- **Quality** (AG News, nearest neighbour of the same category; MiniLM 85.7 %, bag-of-words student 82.3 %, 4,096-word bag 84.9 %): float transformer **84.1 %** (7,168 texts); integer version: int8/float cosine **0.961** and **83.8 %** on a subset of 2,000 texts (float on the same subset: 82.7 %: the difference is sampling noise). On 8 hand-written sentences: 7 out of 8.
- **Exactness on a real chain.** 256 texts → 256 jobs, each verified operation by operation (Freivalds for the 11 GEMMs, exact recomputation of the rest) then paid: **256/256 embeddings byte-for-byte identical** to the independent Python engine (`python3 tools/transformer_demo.py`).
- **Throughput: low, and it is a design limit.** One job = one text: the attention softmax applies to the first `len` rows of a column, without a mask, so several sequences cannot share a tensor without attending to each other. Measured: **≈ 1 text/s** (256 jobs in 189 s on a CPU worker, `n = 128`; 109 s of cumulative verification on the client side), i.e. ~11 VRL of floor escrow per text. The lookup of the token embedding table (`E` + positions) is done by the **client**, off-chain, as for the bag of words; the network runs and verifies everything else.
- **What it is not.** It is not MiniLM (6 layers, 12 heads, `d = 384`, biases, affine LayerNorm, GELU); it is the proof that the protocol's building blocks (attention, LayerNorm, softmax, residuals, FFN) carry a **trained model** with a measured and acceptable fidelity loss. Moving to several layers is mechanical (repeat the block) up to the bounds of 240 operations and 64 weight blocks; several heads require a per-head row selection (a selection weight block per head); the real obstacle is the **attention mask** (sequence batching) and per-channel quantization for deeper models `[RESEARCH]`.

**Wider and deeper version** (same blocks, `train_tiny_transformer.py --d 128 --ff 256 --dout 256 --layers 2`, `models/transformer2-int8.json`): chain `n = 256`, **34 operations including 19 GEMMs, 15 weight blocks**; int8/float cosine **0.981** (against 0.961 for the one-layer version: more representation precision), nearest neighbour **83.8 % int8** (84.2 % float) on 2,000 texts, float student/teacher cosine 0.70 (against 0.61); on a real chain, 256 texts → 256 jobs, **256/256 embeddings byte-for-byte identical** to the independent engine, ≈ 0.7 text/s (362 s, CPU worker). The "nearest neighbour" metric plateaus around 84 % for these sizes: it does not measure everything that depth brings.

Reproduce: `python3 tools/train_tiny_transformer.py WEIGHTS.npz` (~1 min), `python3 tools/quantize_transformer.py WEIGHTS.npz MODEL.json`, `python3 tools/transformer_demo.py MODEL.json`; client: `TransformerEmbedClient` (`embed_client.py`), shipped model `models/tiny-transformer-int8.json` with its float weights `models/tiny-transformer-float.npz`.

## 4 quater. ResNet-20 (CIFAR-10): residual blocks, quantization-aware training, verified on GPU `[MEASURED]`

A **branching** convolutional network (`tools/train_resnet_cifar.py`: 3 stages of 3 blocks, identity shortcuts or stride-2 1×1 convolutions, BatchNorm, global average, dense layer; **88.45 %** in float after 3 min of CPU training) converted by `tools/quantize_graph.py` into **76 operations including 22 GEMMs of 1024³, 23 weight blocks**, one 32×32 image per job (`n = 1024`).

- **`Add`s require tensors on the same scale.** All the tensors linked by additions form a *scale group*: a single scale (percentile of the real range of all the members), and each convolution that produces a member is brought to this **target scale** by adjusting the scale of its weights (integer shift): no rescaling operation, no floating point in the program. The resulting program has 3 groups (one per stage).
- **Post-training quantization: insufficient.** With tensor scales (BatchNorm folded into the weights, 7 bits of activation): **84.6 %** against 90.2 % in float on 500 images (−5.6 points). A sweep shows that *less* clipping is worse (99.99th percentile: 75.7 %, maximum: 48.3 %): it is the resolution of the activations that is lacking, not the range. A correction of the mean bias of the arithmetic shift (−½ notch) **degraded** the result (75 %): abandoned.
- **Quantization-aware fine-tuning** (`tools/qat_graph.py`): the whole integer program is **simulated exactly in torch** (input `round(64x)`, integer products, arithmetic shift then saturation, integer biases, ReLU, saturated `Add`, rounded mean; differentiable roundings by straight-through) with the scales of the quantization plan; only the float weights and biases are adjusted (8 epochs, 3 min). The simulator reproduces the integer engine identically (0.87 against 87.00 % on the same 100 images before fine-tuning). Result: accuracy of the integer program on 2,000 test images never used for calibration **83.6 % → 88.6 %**, i.e. the accuracy of the float model (88.45 %).   | step | cumulative verification | per image | end to end (32 images) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | start | 145 s | ≈ 4.5 s | 165 s |

## 4 quinquies. The complete MiniLM-L6 (all-MiniLM-L6-v2), verified on GPU `[MEASURED]`

## 4 sexies. High-resolution vision: DeiT-Tiny (224 × 224, 1,000 classes), verified on GPU `[MEASURED]`

The ResNet-20 photos are 32 × 32 pixels because an image there occupies one column per pixel (32 × 32 = 1,024 = `n`). A Vision Transformer lays the image out differently: **196 squares of 16 × 16 pixels**, one per column (768 values per square, in rows). `tools/quantize_vit.py` converts **DeiT-Tiny** (`facebook/deit-tiny-patch16-224`, Apache 2.0: 12 layers, 3 heads of 64, `d = 192`, FFN 768, LayerNorm before each block, 1,000 ImageNet classes) into **two chained programs** (layers 0-5, then 6-11 and the classifier: 189 + 197 operations, 39 + 38 weight blocks, under the bounds of 240 and 64 per job).

- **Everything is computed by the chain**, embedding included: the input carries the pixels of the squares (rows 0-767) and a position marker (rows 768-964, value 64); one Gemm projects the squares, a second adds position + CLS token + bias, an Add combines them. The client only cuts the image up. Job B starts with an identity copy (`Shift 0, 0`): the protocol forbids a per-column operation on a job's raw input.
- **New consensus operation `SoftmaxLo`** (`docs/07` §12): with 197 tokens, 26 to 37 % of the attention mass is carried by probabilities < 1/128, which `Softmax` (output in 1/64) rounds to 0 (measured). `SoftmaxLo` gives the fine part (`round(4096 p) − 64 round(64 p)`, in [−32, 32]); the model computes `V·A_high ≫ s + V·A_fine ≫ (s + 6)`, then +1 to recentre the two roundings by default. Independent Python engine, column fraud proof, 2 cases in the conformance vectors (435 blocks), 2,996 random programs without divergence.
- **Preprocessing**: that of the DeiT paper (short side at 256, 224 crop, ImageNet mean/standard deviation): 76.0 % against 65.0 % with the configuration file of the Hugging Face repository (300 images). The re-implemented float model gives the `transformers` logits to within 1.3·10⁻⁵.
- **Accuracy** (top-1 over the 1,000 classes, 500 validation images per set, simulation verified bit for bit against the reference engine):

  | | float | integer, raw quantization | integer, QAT on Imagenette | **integer, QAT on COCO** |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Imagenette (10 classes) | 78.2 % | 19.4 % | 80.6 % | **70.6 %** |
  | Imagewoof (10 dog breeds) | 74.0 % | — | 56.6 % | **66.2 %** |

  Training on the Imagenette classes **specialises** the model (80.6 % on these classes, 56.6 % on never-seen dog breeds): a flattering figure, discarded. The model kept is trained by distillation on 5,000 COCO photos (everyday scenes, no evaluation class): **about 8 points below float, on both sets**, same prediction as the float model in 75 to 78 % of cases. `python3 tools/qat_vit.py OUT.json --data coco --epochs 5 --lr 5e-5` (50 min, 30 CPU cores), `python3 tools/vit_eval.py OUT.params.npz`.
- **On a real chain (A10 GPU)**: 40 photos 224 × 224 → 80 jobs, each operation verified before payment, **70.4 s (1.76 s per photo)**; **8/8 outputs identical** to the independent reference engine; 30/40 correct (`python3 tools/vit_demo.py MODEL.json --n 40`; client `VitClient`, `tools/vit_client.py`).
- **Limits.** 8 points below float (per-channel quantization and longer training or training on more images are the leads); outliers of the residual stream (channel 21 reaches 11 to 25 against ≈ 0.5 typically); the Imagenette, Imagewoof and COCO photos belong to their authors: they serve to measure, not to illustrate the site.

## 4 octies. A small LANGUAGE MODEL that writes, word by word, verified on GPU `[MEASURED]`

`tools/quantize_gpt.py` converts a GPT (normalization before each block; `gpt_neo` or `gpt2`); by default **TinyStories-33M** (`roneneldan/TinyStories-33M`, MIT licence: 4 layers, 16 heads of 48, `d = 768`, FFN 3,072 GELU, vocabulary of 50,257 words, 68.5 M parameters with the word tables; it writes short stories in English).

- **New consensus operation `CausalSoftmax`** (`docs/07` §12, tag 11): in a language model, a word only sees the preceding words. Column `j` = query `q = j mod l`; only the keys `0..q` count (high part `Softmax` or fine part `SoftmaxLo`, probabilities to 1/4096). Column fraud proof, independent Python engines, 2 cases in the conformance vectors (483 blocks), Rust test; 2,994 random programs without divergence (875 use the operation).
- **Choice of the model, measured** (`tools/llm_probe.py`, simulated quantization without training): **GPT-2 (124 M) collapses** (perplexity 1,345 against 99; residual-stream values up to 2,962: its well-known "massive activations"), even with the transformations below (17 % of the next word). TinyStories-33M tolerates them.
- **Exact transformations before quantization** (same function: logits within 2·10⁻⁵ of `transformers`): zero-mean residual stream (the LayerNorm becomes an RMS normalization), gamma/beta folded into the matrices that read them, **orthogonal rotation** of the stream (which fixes the vector 1: outlier dimensions are spread out), **per-query key offset** (`b_k −= u`, `q·u` adjusted by least squares on the maximal score of each query: softmax unchanged, the scores no longer saturate), vocabulary-centred head.
- **Two jobs per generated word**: **BODY** (the 4 layers, a text of ≤ 64 words: 154 operations including 52 GEMMs, 41 weight blocks) and **HEAD** (final LayerNorm + 50 blocks of 1,024 words: 153 operations, 51 blocks; up to 20 texts at a time). The vocabulary biases are **inside the product**: columns 768-1,023 of the weight blocks, multiplied by witness rows (value 64) that the client only puts in the columns of the texts. A `Bias` would add its value to all the columns, including the empty ones, which would pollute the other blocks once placed side by side (`Shift`) and added (design error found by the bit-for-bit comparison, fixed before training).
- **QAT on GPU** (`tools/qat_gpt.py`: simulation identical to the reference engine, 4/4 before and 8/8 after; distillation (KL divergence) toward the probabilities of the original float model on 100,000 story openings, 2 epochs, 42 min). Measurements on 400 validation story openings (25,183 positions):

  | | perplexity | same next word as float |
  |---|---|---|
  | original float model | **2.367** | — |
  | integer, no training | 2.775 | 84.4 % |
  | integer, QAT epoch 1 | 2.454 | 91.5 % |
  | **integer, QAT epoch 2** | **2.424** (+2.4 %) | **92.9 %** |

- **On a real chain (A10 GPU)** (`python3 tools/gpt_demo.py MODEL.json --new 40`; client `GptClient`, `tools/gpt_client.py`): 8 stories written in parallel, **320 words in 730 s** (40 steps; BODY jobs 472 s, HEAD jobs 257 s), i.e. **18 s per step for the 8 stories** (2.3 s per word on average); each job verified operation by operation before payment; last-step logits **bit-for-bit identical** to the independent engine for **8/8** stories. Greedy decoding (maximal integer logit, ties to the smallest identifier). The stories are coherent; they follow the float model's word for word on 68 of the 320 words (at the first different word, the two texts diverge: that is the nature of greedy decoding). Example (chain): *"Once upon a time, there was a little cat named Tom. Tom was a very curious cat. He liked to explore and find new things. One day, Tom found a big, red ball. He was so happy!"*
- **Limits.** 64 words per text (prompt included); small model, specialised in children's stories; no key/value cache: each step recomputes the whole text; the client reads the word table (8-bit integers, in the model file) and chooses the next word; the model file (173 MB) is not versioned: `python3 tools/qat_gpt.py OUT.json` regenerates it (GPU, 42 min). Natural next steps: SmolLM2-135M (requires an RMS normalization and an element-by-element product in the protocol), more than 64 words.

## 4 nonies. Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B: the first model of more than 0.5 billion parameters `[EXPORTER WRITTEN AND TESTED; NOT PUBLISHED]`

**The model.** `Qwen/Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B` (Apache-2.0): 28 layers, 1,024 features, feed-forward 3,072, 16 query heads and 8 key / value heads of 128, RMS normalisation (and one per head on the queries and keys), rotary position embedding (RoPE, `theta = 10^6`), SwiGLU, causal attention, the embedding is the state of the **last token** (the sequence ends with the end-of-text token), then L2 normalisation; 1,024 dimensions. About 0.44 billion weights outside the word table (the word table is read by the client, like the other models).

**What the protocol was missing, and what was added** (`docs/07` §12.2; consensus change, every node must upgrade): four operations. `RmsNorm{x, len, group, shift}` (the normalisation of the model, and per head of 128 rows: the QK-norm; the root has 4 fractional bits because the integer queries have only a few LSB of rms, and `shift` widens the output range because the normalised queries and keys reach 8 sigma), `MulT{a, b, shift}` (`silu(gate) * up`), `MulW{x, w, shift}` (the cos and sin tables of RoPE as weight blocks) and `CausalSoftmaxS{x, len, l, fine, scale}` (the causal softmax whose exponent step is `2^scale` times coarser: the attention logits of this model span more than 20 nats even relative to the sink token, the 8-bit score tensor of `CausalSoftmax` only 11). Everything else is expressed with the operations that already exist (`Gemm`, `GemmAT`, `Lut` for `silu`, `Mul` for the gains, `HeadSpread` / `HeadGather`, `CausalSoftmax`, `Add`, `Bias`). Status: **implemented and tested** in Rust and, independently, in Python (differential fuzz 500 / 500 identical decisions, 1,197 random programs identical tensor by tensor, 616 blocks in the frozen program vectors, `tests/gated.rs` 10 tests, replayable audit 17 / 17, whole Rust suite and 61 Python tests green).

**The prepended token.** Qwen models give a huge state to the first token of the sequence (an attention sink, about 6,200 on one channel against 25 for the other tokens), which would blow the scale of every tensor. The text is therefore prefixed by one fixed token, `<|endoftext|>`, whose keys and values are the same for every text and are carried by the program as constants. `[MEASURED]` on 112 short sentences of 8 topics: the prefixed embeddings have a cosine of **0.971** (minimum 0.867) with the standard ones, topic nearest-neighbour accuracy 80.4 % against 84.8 %, correlation of all pairwise similarities 0.967 (other prefixes: `' The'` 0.972, `'.'` 0.972, `<|im_start|>` 0.921). It is a slightly different embedding space: prefixed and standard Qwen3 embeddings must not be mixed.

**The exporter** (`tools/quantize_qwen3.py`, tests `tools/test_quantize_qwen3.py`: a tiny random Qwen3-like model at n = 128 with the same code paths, agreement with the `transformers` implementation, exactness of the transformations to 1e-9, protocol bounds and independent validator, integer chain against float, and the Rust engine computing the same tensors as the Python engine job by job). 10 chained jobs (9 of 3 layers with 234 to 235 operations and 56 to 57 weight blocks, then one layer with 80 operations; the export file is about 740 MB; texts of up to 128 tokens, one text per chain). What it does:
- exact transformations of the weights, checked in floating point: gains of the norms folded into the following matrices, residual stream rotated by a random Hadamard matrix, rescaling of the queries / keys through the gains of the QK-norm and of the SwiGLU product (bounded: a dead channel would otherwise get an enormous up projection), the feed-forward channels normalised and **sorted by amplitude into tiles that each have their own scales**, block-Hadamard rotation of each tile before the down projection;
- the sink: its column of the residual stream is never read; in each layer its value column is overwritten by a constant, and **all the scores are taken relative to its key** (the key minus the sink's key: the softmax is unchanged, the sink scores 0), with a per-layer exponent step (`CausalSoftmaxS`, `scale` 1 to 3) chosen from the range of the scores;
- RoPE as `MulW` (tables in 1/64) + a signed permutation `Gemm` + `Add`; GQA by two passes of 8 query heads sharing the keys; the first normalisation reads a finer copy of the embedding (it is ten times smaller than what layer 0 writes);
- every `Gemm` floors its output (a bias of -1/2 LSB on every feature): the constant offsets that matter (the sums of the Gemms of the output projection and of the feed-forward, the Hadamard rotation which concentrates the offset on one channel of each tile) are computed and cancelled by the bias rows that already exist, at no cost in operations.

**Quality measured on the integer engine** (`[MEASURED]`, 48 sentences of 8 topics, mean over the sentences; "float" is the same prefixed model in floating point): cosine between the integer and the float embedding **0.77**; topic nearest-neighbour accuracy **85.4 %** (float 89.6 %, chance 12.5 %; 48 sentences: about 5 points of noise); correlation of all the pairwise similarities 0.65. The first complete run gave a cosine of 0.05; what moved it, in order: the attention scores (a +-5.5 nat tensor against logits of +-24 nats: 0.05 to 0.52), the feed-forward ordering by amplitude and per-tile scales (0.52 to 0.71), the RMS norm (integer root without fractional bits: -10 % of amplitude on the queries; clipping of the normalised queries at 4 sigma, they reach 8: 0.71 to 0.75), the floor offsets and the percentiles (0.75 to 0.78). It is a usable embedding for topic retrieval, **not yet a faithful copy of the model**: the remaining error is spread over every site (queries / keys 0.96 to 0.99, attention context 0.8 to 0.95, feed-forward 0.9 to 0.98 per layer) and compounds over 28 layers.

**The client and the execution on a chain** (`tools/qwen3_client.py`, demo `tools/qwen3_demo.py`; `[MEASURED]` on a local chain, n = 1024, one CPU worker, no GPU): the client tokenises, adds the prefix token, looks the tokens up in the word table, rotates and quantises the input, sends the 10 chained jobs (`vearl-cli infer`; each job is verified operation by operation and paid before its output feeds the next one) and computes the final embedding from the verified integer state of the last token. **8 sentences, 80 verified jobs, 402 s (50 s per text, the texts of a step are processed together), all 8 results identical bit for bit to the independent reference engine, every sentence's nearest neighbour of its own topic.** The workers' registry limit was raised from 512 to 1,024 blocks (the model alone holds 528) and the size of the persisted deposit file from 512 MiB to 2 GiB.

**Not done** `[TO DO]`: quantisation-aware training (the main lever left for the quality), a fused gain in `RmsNorm` (one rounding instead of two on the queries and keys), a standard retrieval benchmark, a GPU run (the CPU worker is the slow part), a test on a network with several workers (the model is 530 MiB to deposit with each); until then this model must not be announced as available.

## 4 decies. Models installed in advance (model packs)

A model is a list of n x n int8 weight blocks; a job names them by Merkle root and the worker looks them up in its **registry** (`HasModel` / `PutModel`). Until rc22 the client sent the blocks the worker did not have, once per worker. That does not scale (Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B is 472 MiB; models of tens of billions of parameters will be tens of GiB), so since rc23 the miners hold the models themselves: `node --models-dir DIR` loads every `*.vblocks` pack of DIR into the registry (at start, then every 30 s), `HasModel` answers "present", and the client sends only its input. Blocks that come from packs do not count in the upload cap of the registry.

Pack format `NAME.vblocks`: 8 bytes `VBLOCK01`, n (u32 little-endian), number of blocks (u32 little-endian), then the blocks (n x n int8, row-major, no duplicate). A node ignores a pack built for another n and refuses a block with a value outside +-64. Build one with `tools/make_model_pack.py --n 1024 --out DIR NAME=model.json ...` (it collects every base64 matrix found under a `weights` key and writes the `models.json` list: name, file, bytes, sha256, blocks, url). `vearl-miner models` downloads the packs of that list, checks the SHA-256 and installs them in `models/`. A pack cannot make a miner compute something wrong: a job refers to its weights by Merkle root, so a block that is not the client's is simply never used.

Measured (Windows, n = 1024): the three packs of the testnet (TinyStories language model 93 blocks, DeiT-Tiny 77, Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B 472) are 650 MiB, load in about 2 s and take 649 MiB of memory; a client `infer` against a node that has the pack reports "3/3 weight blocks already present, 0 sent" (test `a_model_pack_installed_in_advance_makes_the_client_upload_nothing`).
