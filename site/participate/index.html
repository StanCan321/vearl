Vearl public testnet · October 23, 2026, 16:00 UTC

Mine the testnet. Be counted for the mainnet.

Everything you need to take part: what to install, how to start, how contributions are counted, and what to do to be considered for the thank-you the team plans for the testnet miners on the mainnet.

Please read first. The testnet tokens have no value and cannot be sold or converted. The reward on the mainnet is a plan of the team, not a promise: no amount is guaranteed, and the rules can change (changes are announced before they apply). Nothing here is investment advice. The code has not been audited by a third party and the network can be reset.

1. What you need

  • A computer with an NVIDIA GPU of the Ampere generation or newer: RTX 30xx, 40xx or 50xx, A-series, L-series, H100. Without a GPU the miner runs on the CPU, but very slowly.
  • The NVIDIA driver: Windows 580 or newer, Linux 575 or newer.
  • Windows 10 or 11 (64-bit), or Linux 64-bit with glibc 2.35 or newer (for example Ubuntu 22.04 or later).
  • An Internet connection. The miner connects to the seed node and downloads the chain, which takes a few minutes the first time.
  • A pen and a sheet of paper: the wallet gives you 24 recovery words that you must write down.

2. Download and start

  1. Download the archive for your system from the Releases page on GitHub: the windows-x86_64.zip or the linux-x86_64.tar.gz. Download only from there. Check the SHA-256 against the SHA256SUMS.txt file of the release if you want to be sure.
  2. Extract it anywhere. On Windows, double-click vearl-miner.exe. On Linux, open a terminal in the folder and run ./vearl-miner.
  3. The first time, an assistant creates your wallet. It shows 24 recovery words once: write them on paper. They are the only backup of your wallet. Never photograph them, never type them anywhere, and never give them to anybody: nobody from the project will ever ask for them.
  4. Leave the window open. The miner starts mining as soon as the testnet opens, and prints a status line every 10 seconds (it is also saved in miner.log).

Vearl Wallet (vearl-wallet-gui) is in the same folder: a wallet with a window, to see your balance, receive, send and register for the rewards. Double-click it on Windows, or run it from a desktop session on Linux. It lists the wallet.json that the miner made, and it can also create a wallet or restore one from its 24 words.

The miner mines Vearl only. An optional add-on can also mine Quantus (QTC) with the same GPU, but it is off by default and does not count for the testnet rewards.

Windows may warn about an unknown program (the files are not code-signed yet) and some antivirus programs flag any miner. You can read the source code and build it yourself: github.com/vearl-verified-compute/vearl.

3. Check that you are mining

Your wallet address starts with vrl1 and is shown by the miner at start. After the testnet opens:

  • the status line shows blocks found going up (with one machine it can take a while: blocks are a lottery, and your share of them follows your share of the computing power);
  • the explorer shows every block, job and miner;
  • the leaderboard shows your points, computed only from the blocks of the chain. Paste your address in its search box.

4. How contributions are counted

The team plans to set aside 5 % of the mainnet supply to thank the people who help secure the testnet, shared between the eligible contributors in proportion to their points. Points come only from the blocks that your address mined: nobody can inflate them off-chain, and anybody can recompute the result from the published blocks.

A valid block
1 point
A block that carried paid work
1.5 points (a real job from a client)
Early period
first 3 days after the start: points ×1.25
Several machines
allowed, with the same address or not: your total is the sum of your points

To be eligible for the thank-you you must also:

  • be active on at least 3 different days (24-hour windows since the start) and hold at least 0.005 % of all the points;
  • register your address with a signature that proves you own it (next section);
  • play fair: no fraud (a wrong result published by your worker, forged registrations) and no attack on the network outside the rules of the security policy;
  • be one participant per person, of legal age, in a country that the team is allowed to reward (the list will be published before registration opens), and accept the terms.

After the testnet, the team takes a final snapshot, publishes the blocks, the script and the list of excluded addresses with reasons, and leaves 14 days to contest a result. The plan is to deliver the reward at the mainnet launch, partly at once and the rest over several months. The full rules are in docs/19.

5. Register your address

Registration is one command: the wallet signs a message that proves that you own your address, shows it to you, and, after you confirm, sends it by e-mail to register@vearl.org. A signature proves ownership without revealing any secret. Registration opens during the testnet: the date will be announced on the Discord and on X.

The easy way: open Vearl Wallet, choose your wallet, go to Rewards, type your Discord name or your e-mail address, press Sign the registration (it asks your password), check the three lines it shows and press Send the registration. The command line below does exactly the same thing.

  1. Open a terminal in the miner folder (Windows: click the address bar of the folder, type cmd and press Enter). Your wallet file is wallet.json, in that folder.
  2. Run this, with your Discord name or your e-mail address instead of the capitals (the wallet asks your password): vearl-wallet register --file wallet.json --contact "YOUR_DISCORD_NAME_OR_EMAIL"
  3. The wallet shows you the three lines it will send (address, message, signature) and asks Send it now? [y/N]. Type y.
  4. Within a few minutes your address appears in the public list of registered addresses (addresses only, never your contact). The leaderboard then marks it as registered.

If the wallet cannot send the e-mail (some networks block it), it tells you why and shows the three lines: send them yourself in an e-mail to register@vearl.org. You can also run the command with --print-only to only show them. On Linux, use ./vearl-wallet in the same way. This needs the release that includes the register command.

Nobody will ever ask for your 24 words, your password or your wallet file. The only address that receives registrations is register@vearl.org.

Good to know

Do the testnet tokens have a value?
No. They cannot be bought, sold or converted. They only exist to test the network.
Can the team or anybody ask for my 24 words or my password?
Never. Anyone who asks is a scammer, including a message that seems to come from the team. We will not contact you first about your wallet.
What if I lose my 24 words?
Your wallet and the points linked to that address cannot be recovered. Write them on paper and keep them safe.
Can I use a pool?
The blocks are what is counted, so mine with your own address with the official miner. Pool mining is documented in the repository for people who run pools.
Can the testnet be reset?
Yes. Your points are kept: the team publishes the blocks and the leaderboard data at each snapshot.
Where do I ask a question?
On the Discord, or write to contact@vearl.org. To report a security problem, write to security@vearl.org and do not post it publicly.
